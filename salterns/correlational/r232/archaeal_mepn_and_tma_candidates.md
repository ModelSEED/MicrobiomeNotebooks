# Archaeal MePn consumers and TMA-route candidates: what the annotations support

Generated by `make_archaeal_mepn_tma_report.py` on 2026-10-26. Gene coordinates and RAST function strings for every hit are in `archaeal_mepn_and_tma_candidates_genes.csv`.

**Scope.** The archaea in either MePn set:
- the two *phnJ* carriers, Natrinema.1 and Haloquadratum.4;
- Halorubrum.3, which is in the hand-curated 2020 set only.

No other archaeal MAG is in either set. The report also covers the four TMA-route candidates (UBA11579.1, J153.1, CAZFJG01.2, T1Sed10-7.1). Methanolobus.1 is added as context, because it is the only methanogen that could turn TMA into CH₄.

Everything below comes from the RAST annotation (function strings and gene locations) and from sequence comparisons on the RAST protein translations. No profile-HMM search, phylogeny or experiment was run. Statements marked *inference* go beyond what the annotation states.

## Bottom line

1. **Natrinema.1 and Haloquadratum.4 both carry a C-P lyase locus that, at the annotation level, is complete enough to release CH₄ from MPn.**
   - Both loci hold the PhnCDE transporter and PhnH, I, J, K, L and M. The arrangement is the same in both: *phnDCE* is divergent from *phnGHIJKLM*, followed by one unannotated gene.
   - RAST did not call PhnG in either MAG. The gene at the PhnG position is still a PhnG homologue (*inference*). It scores 114 and 110 against RAST-annotated PhnG from other MAGs; random proteins score at most 57. The two copies are 59% identical to each other.
   - Across the two loci, matching genes are 53%–81% identical; PhnJ is 81%.
   - In Haloquadratum.4 the whole locus is on one contig. In Natrinema.1 it is split at *phnI* across two contigs, whose ends overlap by 124 nt at 94% identity.
   - What is missing: *phnN* in both (*phnP* is annotated elsewhere in both genomes). PhnN only recycles the ribose product and is not needed to release CH₄.
2. **Halorubrum.3 has no C-P lyase.**
   - Its 2020 membership rests on one lone *phnM* gene among unrelated genes. That gene is a real PhnM homologue, 49%–52% identical to the two operon PhnMs. It also has generic PhnC-type and "cluster 12" ABC transporter genes and a PhnP-labelled metallo-β-lactamase.
   - PhnM alone cannot cleave the C–P bond. The *phnJ* rule is therefore right to exclude Halorubrum.3.
3. **None of the 3 archaea was modelled in 2020.**
   - None is among the 20 MAGs with MePn → CH₄ simulations (`saltern_fluxes.json`), and none has a model in `models/MePn_consumers/`.
   - Their base ModelSEED models (`correlational/models/*.RAST.json`) do contain C-P lyase reactions, found by the compound IDs cpd25960 (MePn), cpd26518 (RPnTP) and cpd26519 (PRPn):
     - Haloquadratum.4: rxn26456 (MePn → RPnTP; phnI CDS.671 and phnL CDS.674); rxn26457 (RPnTP → PRPn; phnM CDS.675); rxn26459 (PRPn → CH₄; phnJ CDS.672). That is the whole intracellular MePn → CH₄ chain, each step with a gene rule.
     - Natrinema.1: rxn26456 (MePn → RPnTP; (phnI CDS.1962 or phnI CDS.1017) and phnL CDS.1959); rxn26459 (PRPn → CH₄; phnJ CDS.1961). rxn26457 (RPnTP → PRPn) is missing: RAST labels its PhnM "Metal-dependent hydrolase involved in phosphonate metabolism" (CDS.1958), without EC 3.6.1.63.
     - Halorubrum.3: rxn26457 (RPnTP → PRPn; phnM CDS.254). Nothing else of the chain.
     - None of the 3 has an MePn uptake reaction (no extracellular MePn, cpd25960_e0).
   - These base-model reactions may be how the hand-curated 2020 set came to include all 3 archaea, Halorubrum.3 through rxn26457 alone (*inference*, untested).
   - Both *phnJ* archaea live in the unrestored cores:
     - Natrinema.1: mean 0.279 % of reads there, against 0.013 % (restored) and 0.0006 % (reference).
     - Haloquadratum.4: 0.306 % in R2_A, and ≤ 0.007 % in every other core.
4. **The annotations do not support TMA production by any of the four TMA-route candidates.**
   - None of the 310 MAGs has CutC, CutD or CntB annotated. None of the four candidates has a glycyl-radical enzyme, Tmm, TorA/TorZ or TMA dehydrogenase.
   - The TMA-forming enzymes the annotations do show are betaine reductase protein B (GrdH/GrdI), in the 9 betaine reducers (a separate guild; table in section 2), and TMAO reductase: TorA/TorZ-type labels in 7 MAGs, plus DMSO-reductase genes with a dual TMAO-reductase label in 2 more (DATFIX01.1, Halorubrum.3). None of the four TMA candidates carries either enzyme.
   - The three "clustered with choline TMA-lyase" hits are shell or EutJ-like genes of bacterial microcompartment (BMC) loci.
     - In CAZFJG01.2 the labelled gene shares its contig with ethanolamine ammonia-lyase (EutBC) and other Eut/Pdu genes, which points to an ethanolamine, not choline, BMC (*inference*).
     - In T1Sed10-7.1 EutBC is on a different contig from the labelled gene, in a bin with 25.5 % contamination, so the BMC type of the labelled locus is unresolved.
     - In UBA11579.1 the locus runs off the contig edge.
   - J153.1 has one CntA-labelled oxygenase, with no CntB-labelled gene in the genome and no reductase next to it. Its other annotations describe routes that break choline, betaine and carnitine down without TMA, but by the same standard these are incomplete too:
     - choline → betaine → dimethylglycine → sarcosine → glycine: GbcA is likewise a lone Rieske-oxygenase subunit (no GbcB, which is annotated only in HIMB30.1, Halomonas.1 and Marinobacterium_E.2), and no dimethylglycine → sarcosine enzyme is annotated;
     - carnitine 3-dehydrogenase.
5. **CAZFJG01.2 (2020 ID Synergistales.2) is the only one of the four in the 2020 betaine models.**
   - Its modelled TMA flux of 0.1 comes from `rxn17220_c0` ("Betaine reductase"). That reaction has **no gene association**. It equals the betaine uptake bound (0.1, set in the 2020 medium), which the solution reaches.
   - The genome encodes glycine-reductase protein B genes on 5 contigs, but no betaine-type GrdH. Against the B proteins of other MAGs, 7 of its 7 B-protein paralogs (≥ 150 aa) score higher with glycine-type GrdB/GrdE than with betaine-type GrdH/GrdI.
6. **Where the candidates live.**
   - The two candidates with the most TMA-like annotations, J153.1 and UBA11579.1, are reference-wetland organisms:
     - J153.1: mean 0.372 % in R2A, ≈ 0.009 % elsewhere;
     - UBA11579.1: mean 0.141 % in R2A, 0.0003 % in unrestored cores.
   - R2A is the site with net CH₄ uptake, so these two are unlikely TMA suppliers for methanogenesis in the unrestored cores (*inference*).
7. **The TMA → CH₄ step itself is genomically present.**
   - Methanolobus.1 encodes pyrrolysine-containing MttB, MtbB and MtmB (TMA, DMA and MMA methyltransferases), their corrinoid proteins, MtbA and the pyrrolysine machinery.
   - The missing link is a demonstrated TMA source, not the methanogen.

## How the inventory was made

**Gene rules.** The `RULES` regular expressions are matched against each RAST feature's function string, so a label is only as specific as the string. Some labels are broader:
- *phnCDE-like ABC (cluster 12)*, *phnP* and *CaiB/BaiF family* are generic;
- *FMO, generic*: Tmm is a flavin-containing monooxygenase, but so are many other enzymes;
- *MttB superfamily, non-pyl* is the bacterial non-pyrrolysine family. Its characterised members include a glycine betaine:corrinoid methyltransferase (Ticak et al. 2014, *PNAS* 111:E4668), which demethylates betaine rather than making TMA.

**Coordinates** are 1-based and inclusive, with start ≤ stop; strand is given separately. *gap* is the intergenic distance to the previous gene on the contig (negative = overlap). *gene_on_contig* (CSV) gives the gene's rank on its contig. *at_contig_edge* means the gene ends within 30 bp of a contig end, so it may be truncated.

**Recoded genes.** RAST reads an in-frame Sec (UGA) or Pyl (UAG) codon as a stop, so such a gene can appear as two ORFs. Where a "selenocysteine-" or "pyrrolysine-containing" ORF ends at that codon and its in-frame read-through ends at the stop of the next ORF, the two ORFs are counted as one gene; the CSV `evidence` column marks both.

**Sequence checks.** Local Smith–Waterman alignment of RAST protein translations (BLOSUM62, gap −11/−1).
- The null is 500 random proteins (> 80 aa) from the 310 MAGs: "null 99th / max" is that distribution's 99th percentile and maximum score.
- Reference sets are the RAST-annotated proteins of the 310 MAGs: PhnG n = 21, PhnN n = 21, PhnP n = 162, PhnM n = 34, PhnJ n = 27; GrdH 22, GrdI 10, GrdB 42, GrdE 22. A query is never compared with genes from its own MAG.
- Raw scores are not E-values. A score well above the null maximum is good evidence of homology; a profile-HMM search would be the proper test.

**Abundance** is the relative abundance of reads in `data/Cliff_310MAG_relabund.tsv`, with the two depths merged by DNA weighting as in the R232 rerun. It is shown in % of reads. *Detected* means > 1e-5.

**RAST domain.** 8 of the 8 genome objects examined here carry `domain: "B"` (genetic code 11), including all 4 archaeal ones, so the archaea were annotated with bacterial settings. RAST k-mer calls can also miss divergent archaeal homologues, as PhnG shows here.

### Natrinema.1

**Taxonomy and quality.**
- *Natrinema hispanicum_A*: ANI 98.63 % to GCF_900111485.1.
- CheckM 82.0 % complete; the bin is fragmented (509 contigs, N50 6.1 kb).

**Abundance.** Mean 0.279 % in the unrestored cores (maximum 0.698 % in R1_B), and at most 0.013 % on average at the restored and reference sites.

**C-P lyase locus.** It spans two contigs:
- the first holds *phnM*-like – *phnL* – *phnK* – *phnJ* – the 3′ part of *phnI*, all on the − strand;
- the second holds the 5′ part of *phnI* – *phnH* – the unannotated PhnG candidate, also on −, with *phnD* – *phnC* – *phnE* divergent on +.

The two *phnI* fragments are probably one gene split by the assembly:
- the fragment on `NODE_4887 (8.7 kb)` is 100 aa and sits at a contig edge;
- the fragment on `NODE_9021 (5.6 kb)` is 331 aa and also sits at a contig edge;
- stitching the contigs at the ends of their alignment and translating across the junction gives one 389-aa PhnI ORF, in which the two fragments overlap by 42 aa (Haloquadratum.4's intact PhnI is 383 aa);
- the contig ends align over 124 nt at 94.4% identity (4887 start against 9021 end, same strand).

The nucleotide overlap is not exact. Of its 7 mismatches, 6 are synonymous and 1 changes the protein, so the 42-aa protein overlap differs at 1 residue (residue 61: A in the NODE_9021 copy, T in the NODE_4887 copy). That fits contigs from two closely related haplotypes in the bin (*inference*). Read pairs spanning the junction would settle it.

Two genes in the locus carry no RAST PhnG/PhnM label:
- The *phnM* position holds "Metal-dependent hydrolase involved in phosphonate metabolism", 71% identical to Haloquadratum.4's RAST-annotated PhnM (EC 3.6.1.63).
- The PhnG position holds a 147-aa "hypothetical protein". It overlaps *phnH* by 4 bp on the same strand and scores 114 against RAST PhnG (null maximum 57; best against PhnN, PhnP or PhnM ≤ 61).

PhnJ is 56% identical (over 272 aa) to the closest bacterial PhnJ in the 310 MAGs (Saliniramus.1).

**Other genes.**
- A *phnP*-labelled metallo-β-lactamase is on another contig.
- No *phnF* or *phnN*, and no phosphonatase (*phnW*/*phnX*), *phnY*/*phnZ*, *mpnS* or *pepM*.
- The "carboxyvinyl-carboxyphosphonate phosphorylmutase" (a PEP-mutase-family label) sits on an unrelated contig with no phosphonate-biosynthesis neighbours. It is not evidence for MPn synthesis.
- Pi genes: *pstS* ×3, *pstC* ×1, *pstA* ×2, *pstB* ×1, and 3 "phosphate regulatory protein homolog". No *phoB*/*phoR* is annotated.

**Verdict.** All the subunits that make CH₄ from MPn are present (PhnG by inference): uptake (PhnCDE), RPnTP synthesis (PhnG/H/I/L), PhnM hydrolysis of RPnTP to PRPn, and the C–P lyase PhnJ; a role for PhnK was not established in vitro. The biochemistry is in Kamat, Williams & Raushel 2011, *Nature* 480:570.
Two caveats:
- the locus is split across contigs;
- whether haloarchaeal C-P lyase is expressed and active in situ is untested.

### Haloquadratum.4

**Taxonomy and quality.**
- *Haloquadratum walsbyi*: ANI 98.39 % to GCF_000237865.1.
- CheckM 83.5 % complete; 376 contigs, N50 9.3 kb.

**Abundance.** Almost all of it is in one core: 0.306 % in R2_A, the core whose D2 sample the bin came from. Every other core has ≤ 0.007 %, and it is detected in 6/12 cores.

**C-P lyase locus.** One contig, `NODE_822 (19.2 kb)`, carries the whole locus.
- *phnD* – *phnC* – *phnE* – *phnO*-like acetyltransferase run on the − strand.
- Divergent on +: the PhnG candidate – *phnH* – *phnI* – *phnJ* – *phnK* – *phnL* – *phnM* – an unannotated gene.
- From the PhnG candidate to *phnM* the genes overlap or abut: gaps of -8 to 3 bp.

The PhnG candidate is a 149-aa gene with no RAST function. It scores 110 against RAST PhnG (null maximum 49) and is 59% identical to Natrinema.1's candidate. PhnJ is 53% identical to the closest bacterial PhnJ (Marivita.3).

**Other genes.**
- A second phosphonate-type ABC transporter (*phnC*/*phnD* plus a "cluster 12" permease) on another contig.
- *phnP* elsewhere.
- Pi genes: *pstS* ×1, *pstC* ×1 (at a contig edge) and *phoU* ×2. No *pstA*/*pstB* is annotated, which may reflect the 83.5 % completeness (*inference*).

**Verdict.** This is the most complete and best-assembled archaeal C-P lyase locus: CDE plus GHIJKLM on one contig, colinear with Natrinema.1. It is plausible as an MPn → CH₄ route.

### Halorubrum.3

**Taxonomy and quality.**
- *Halorubrum* sp.; GTDB-Tk assigns no species. The placement neighbour is GCF_051194135.1 at 89.58 % ANI, which suggests an undescribed species (*inference*).
- CheckM 94.8 % / 1.6 %; N50 20.1 kb.

**Abundance.** Mean 0.159 % in the unrestored cores (maximum 0.309 % in R2_A).

**phn genes.**
- The only C-P lyase-pathway gene is a lone *phnM* (EC 3.6.1.63; 339 aa) on `NODE_93 (55.8 kb)`, between unrelated genes.
- It is 52% identical to Natrinema.1's PhnM, 49% identical to Haloquadratum.4's PhnM (null maximum 95), so it is a genuine PhnM-family homologue.
- No PhnG, H, I, J, K or L is annotated, and no contig carries a C-P lyase cluster.
- The phosphonate-type ABC genes (one *phnC*-labelled ATPase plus a "cluster 12" permease) and the *phnP*-labelled metallo-β-lactamase are generic.
- Pi genes: *pstS*, *pstC*, *pstA*, *pstB* ×2 each and *phoU* ×2, i.e. two Pst systems.

**Verdict.** Not an MPn → CH₄ organism by annotation.
- Without PhnJ and the PhnGHIL complex, PhnM has no substrate.
- At 95 % completeness, losing a whole seven-gene operon to incompleteness is possible but unlikely (*inference*).
- The lone PhnM could be a relic of a lost operon or serve another function (*inference*, unverified).

## 2. TMA-route candidates

These MAGs were flagged by `tma_producer` in `guilds_r232.csv`: either a CntA annotation, or a gene RAST calls "... clustered with choline trimethylamine-lyase".

Known TMA-forming routes and what each needs:
- **Choline → TMA:** the glycyl-radical enzyme CutC plus its activase CutD, usually inside a BMC (Craciun & Balskus 2012, *PNAS* 109:21307).
- **Carnitine → TMA:** the two-component Rieske oxygenase CntA/CntB (Zhu et al. 2014, *PNAS* 111:4268).
- **Betaine → TMA:** the selenoprotein betaine reductase, protein B GrdH/GrdI.
- **TMAO → TMA:** reduction by TorA/TorZ.

CutC belongs to the pyruvate formate-lyase (PFL) family of glycyl-radical enzymes. A CutC that RAST failed to recognise would therefore most likely be labelled "Pyruvate formate-lyase": 66 of the 310 MAGs carry such a glycyl-radical enzyme label, but none of the four candidates does. Across the 310 MAGs, the counts carrying a CutC, CutD, CntB, Tmm or TMA-dehydrogenase label are 0, 0, 0, 0 and 0.

**For comparison: the 9 betaine reducers (*grdH*).** Betaine reductase turns glycine betaine into TMA and acetyl phosphate. It is one of two TMA-forming enzymes annotated in this MAG set. The other is TMAO reductase: TorA/TorZ-type labels in 7 MAGs (Aequoribacter.1, JAACFI01.1, JAHEIH01.1, JASBRS01.1, Roseovarius.10, Roseovarius.11, UBA3439.1), and a dual "@ ... trimethylamine-N-oxide reductase" label on DMSO-reductase genes in 2 more (DATFIX01.1, Halorubrum.3). None of the four TMA candidates carries either enzyme. The betaine reducers were not examined gene by gene here; for the pyl-MttB column, see the end of the Methanolobus.1 section.

## What would test these readings

**MePn (Natrinema.1, Haloquadratum.4).**
1. **Confirm the gene calls.** Run profile-HMM searches (Pfam/TIGRFAM or KEGG KOfam) over the loci, especially the PhnG candidates and the gene after *phnM*. Then place PhnJ in a phylogeny with characterised bacterial PhnJ.
2. **Check that the locus is not a binning artefact.** Look for the same locus in the closest reference genomes (GCF_900111485.1, GCF_000237865.1). Map read pairs across the Natrinema.1 *phnI* junction.
3. **Test expression.** Look for archaeal *phnJ* transcripts in the unrestored cores (metatranscriptome or RT-qPCR), and measure porewater Pi. In bacteria, C-P lyase is induced by Pi limitation; regulation in haloarchaea is not established (unverified).
4. **Test activity.** Amend unrestored cores with ¹³C-MPn, with and without added Pi, and measure ¹³CH₄. If an isolate of the reference species is available, test growth on MPn as the sole P source with CH₄ in the headspace.
5. **Modelling.** If MePn modelling is rerun:
   - Haloquadratum.4's base model already has rxn26456, rxn26457 and rxn26459 with gene rules. Natrinema.1's lacks only rxn26457, which its phnM-like gene (CDS.1958) would carry.
   - Neither has MePn uptake. The 24 curated models in `models/MePn_consumers/` add it as a gene-less "MePn ABC Transporter" (rxs00001; all 24) with an MePn exchange; here it could be tied to the PhnCDE genes. For the C-P lyase step they use a gene-less reaction, rxs43120 (all 24), in place of rxn26459.
   - Drop Halorubrum.3.

**TMA (the four candidates).**
1. **Search the full assemblies, not just the bins.** Look for CutC/glycyl-radical domains and CntB-type reductases with HMMs, and extend the UBA11579.1 BMC contig in the assembly graph to find the locus's signature enzyme.
2. **Place the ambiguous genes phylogenetically.**
   - J153.1's CntA-like protein, alongside characterised CntA and other Rieske oxygenases;
   - CAZFJG01.2's GrdB paralogs, alongside characterised glycine-, sarcosine- and betaine-specific protein B.
   - Then compare with its 99.8 %-ANI reference genome, GCA_964410915.1.
3. **Incubations.** Add ¹³C-choline, ¹³C-carnitine and ¹³C-betaine to anoxic slurries of unrestored cores, with and without BES (a methanogenesis inhibitor, so TMA accumulates), and measure ¹³C-TMA and ¹³CH₄. Pair this with metatranscriptomics of *cutC*, *cntA* and *grd* genes.
4. **Modelling.** Until a TMA-forming gene is found, the betaine → TMA flux attributed to CAZFJG01.2 should be described as assumed.

## Caveats

- **RAST labels.** Function strings come from RAST k-mers (2020), and the archaea were annotated with domain "B". Missing labels for divergent archaeal genes are expected; PhnG is the clear case here.
- **Absence of a gene.** The bins are 78–100 % complete, so a missing gene is not proof the organism lacks it.
- **Alignment scores.** These are local-alignment scores against a random-protein null, not E-values, and positional homology is an inference.
- **Abundance.** It is relative read abundance (DNA-weighted depth merge), not activity, and it is bounded by binning: unbinned reads are not counted.
- **2020 sets.** The 2020 MePn and betaine sets were hand-curated; their gene criteria are not recorded beyond what `guilds_r232.csv` reconstructs.
